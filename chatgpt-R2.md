# R2 — Publisher playbooks & KPI gates (researched 2026-10-02, tool: browser)

## Summary  
- **Typical pipeline:** Publishers universally follow a staged funnel: ideation/concept → concept-video test on social (CTR/IPM) → prototype UA test (measure D1/D7 retention, CPI, playtime, ARPDAU) → soft launch → scale or kill. Early tests use small budgets (often a few $/day) to gauge core appeal; full soft-launch campaigns often run at $500–1,000/day until performance stabilizes.  
- **KPI focus:** Hypercasual and hybrid publishers emphasize different KPIs.  For hypercasual, marketability (low CPI, high IPM) is critical: e.g. Unity’s Supersonic advises CPI <$0.30 (global benchmark) and IPM (installs per 1K impressions) as key early signals.  For hybrid/puzzle games, retention and LTV matter more: high Day‑1 and Day‑7 retention (often 40–50% D1 and >10% D7 in top games) are prized. Overall, publishers will kill concepts with very low retention (e.g. D1<25%), and only scale titles with retention/LTV well above market averages.  
- **Sample size and budget:** Soft-launch guides suggest reading retention only when you have “readable” cohort sizes (typically thousands of installs per test).  For example, one guide’s kill criteria use cohort thresholds (D1 on cohorts large enough to be statistically significant).  In practice, small teams with ~$1K budgets might only achieve a few thousand installs per variant – enough to estimate D1 but too noisy for D30 or ROAS. Such mini-tests can confirm core loop appeal but usually cannot reliably predict long-term LTV without much more spend (sample-size guidance is not found in the sources).  
- **Iteration on failures:** If a prototype “misses” (e.g. D1 or D7 retention below target), publishers first fix the core funnel (onboarding, tutorials, first levels).  Next, they “deepen the meta-layer”: add more content/levels or progression systems to improve D7/D30 retention.  If playtime is low, adding content or difficulty gating can increase session length.  In short, retention lapses are addressed by redesigning early levels and adding meta-progression, then monetization is layered in once retention is stable.  
- **Puzzle-specific metrics:** Puzzle/hybrid games rely more on sustained engagement and IAPs than pure hypercasuals.  Industry analysts note “solid retention rates” and robust ad revenue in puzzle games, and major titles combine ad and IAP monetization.  Thus metrics like D7/D30 retention, ARPDAU or ARPPU, and IAP conversion become critical for puzzles (rather than only CTR/IPM).  No concrete numeric thresholds for puzzle genres were found; general benchmarks for casual games (e.g. top-5 puzzle) are modestly higher retention than average (puzzle’s peer D1 ≈28–32% on Android).  In practice, publishers treat puzzle games more like hybrid-casual: they seek higher retention and LTV rather than extreme CPI/IPM values.  
- **Benchmarks (2024–26):** Broad reports suggest typical casual game retention on Android is ~20–25% D1 (market median), with *top-quartile* titles at 25–32% D1.  Day-7 retention medians are only ~3–4%.  In puzzle/casual genres (AppsFlyer 2022), D1 is ~28–32% and D7 median ~3–4%.  By comparison, hybrid/puzzle hits often deliver D1≈40–50% and D7≈10–15%.  CPI benchmarks vary: Supersonic cites global CPI < $0.30 as healthy for hypercasual; in practice tier‑1 CPIs for hypercasual are often ~$0.20–$0.50, but puzzle games (with IAP) can tolerate much higher CPI if LTV is strong.  Precise country-tier CPI or ROAS by genre were not found in the open sources.  
- **Publisher examples (process & budgets):**  For instance, Unity’s ironSource blog outlines a 5-step launch: (1) concept-video test on social (marketing creative), (2) concept-video on ad networks, (3) integrate ads/IAP in prototype, (4) soft-launch in test markets, (5) global scale.  It recommends capping early campaigns at ~$500–1000/day, finding a “sweet spot” in ~3–5 days, then expanding gradually.  Soft-launch review cycles (from GameGrowthAdvisor) propose iterating in 2-week builds: test core loop (halt if D1<25%), then retention (add meta), then monetization (check LTV vs CPI), then full-scale test.  Rollic (Zynga) famously ideates ~1000 prototypes/month and quickly kills weak ones; only a tiny fraction move from soft-launch to global scale.  Across publishers, early “marketability” tests (CTR/IPM of concept videos) often require ~1000–2000 impressions to judge viability, while prototypes may need several thousand installs to read retention (specific numbers were not given in sources).  
- **Solo dev with $100–1500:**  With only a few hundred to ~$1500 for UA, a solo can run basic tests: e.g. use Meta/TikTok app ads for low budget concept tests (a few hundred installs to measure CTR/IPM), then spend remaining on a prototype soft test (perhaps a couple thousand installs).  This is generally enough to gauge D1 retention (and kill obviously bad concepts), but too small to trust D7/LTV or ROAS data reliably.  Critical disclaimers: sources emphasize that market-level KPIs assume sizable cohorts, and point out that D1 below ~20–25% typically dooms paid scaling.  With limited budget, one must interpret metrics cautiously: a modest concept test can confirm “hooks” that generate CTR/IPM above Facebook benchmarks (often ~2–5% CTR), but will not prove profitability.  In sum, with ~$1K one can weed out the worst ideas (very low CTR/IPM or D1), but cannot scale or make ROI claims without much larger tests (no authoritative guidance for solo budgets was found).  
- **Draft-gate check (user’s gates):**  Published data roughly supports some kill thresholds (e.g. D1<25% kill), but the user’s scale criteria are more aggressive than typical. For example, aiming D1≥40% and D7≥15% is above normal puzzle benchmarks (peer D1 is ~28–32%, D7 top ~7–8%).  Similarly, targets like LTV:CPI>1.3 are minimal (industry often cites LTV:CPI ~3:1 as healthy).  The table below compares each gate to evidence:

## 3. Sample size, installs, and budget thresholds  

No publisher gave explicit install-count or budget rules.  A GameGrowthAdvisor soft-launch framework implies testing on “cohorts large enough to be readable”.  For example, it warns that *D1 below 25% on Android should stop a test immediately* (implying one must accumulate enough installs to measure D1 reliably, though no number is stated).  Informally, industry practitioners often treat *~1,000 installs per ad creative* as a minimal sanity check (yielding ~250 retained players if D1=25%) – but no authoritative source is cited.  In practice, solo developers with a few hundred dollars may only reach a few thousand installs (which can give a D1 signal but not solid D30).  **No explicit “minimum installs” or budget thresholds were found in the connected sources.** (All budget guidance was qualitative, e.g. “cap at \$500–1,000/day” during soft launch, not total spend.)

## 4. Prototype iteration (what to change first if metrics miss)  

Publishers universally first **improve the core loop/onboarding**.  For example, if Day‑1 retention is below the kill line, GameGrowthAdvisor advises stopping UA and fixing first-session friction.  Next, if deeper retention (D7–D30) is weak, publishers “deepen the meta layer”: add more levels or progression.  As Azur Games notes, if many players are dropping off at the last level, the fix is to “add another 10–20 levels” and see if retention improves.  Azur’s classification shows **borderline retention games** (e.g. R1~35%, R7~7%) can be rescued by iterative fixes (level tuning, difficulty).  In summary: *first optimize onboarding (sessions 1–3) if D1 is too low, then iterate level design/length to shore up D7/D30 retention, then adjust ad frequency or monetization placement once engagement is stable.*  (If CPI is problematic, publishers often prioritize retention/LTV fixes first rather than killing for CPI alone.)

## 5. Metrics for puzzle games specifically  

Puzzle and hybrid-casual titles are measured on more **mid/long-term engagement** than pure hyper-casuals.  Industry commentary notes puzzle games enjoy “solid retention rates” and a healthy mix of ad + IAP revenue.  Accordingly, metrics like D7/D30 retention, session length (playtime), ARPDAU or ARPPU, and IAP conversion are especially important for puzzles.  By contrast, hyper-casual play often ends quickly after a few minutes.  No publisher source gave puzzle-specific numeric gates; however, the implication is that **puzzle games need higher retention and LTV**.  For example, one analyst cites that puzzle’s genre growth is driven by broad appeal and stickiness.  In practice, a puzzle game would likely aim for retention closer to the “top games” band (e.g. D1>30%, D7>10%), and would tolerate higher CPI in exchange for high LTV through IAPs.  In short: *for puzzle titles, D1/D7 retention and monetization depth matter more than raw IPM or sub-$0.30 CPI. Metrics like D7 retention and LTV:CPI (not just D1) become deciding factors.*

## 6. Benchmarks 2024–2026 (puzzle/casual)  

Comprehensive benchmark reports specifically for puzzle games were not found in the open sources.  The available data suggests: **Retention:** broad-market median D1 on Android is ~22%, top-quartile ~25–27%.  Puzzle/casual peer D1 was ~28–32% in 2022 (AppsFlyer).  Day‑7 median retention is only ~3–4% (top games ~7–8%).  **CPI:** Specific CPI by region/tier for puzzles was not published; as a reference, Unity/Supersonic cites a global target ~\$0.30 CPI for hyper-casual.  We infer that tier-1 CPIs for casual/puzzle might often range \$0.20–\$1.00 depending on ad format and country (no exact figures given).  **ROAS:** Published industry ranges (Playio) are ~15–25% D7 ROAS for IAP-driven F2P games and ~7–8% for ad-driven casual.  Hybrid/puzzle games often target front-loaded ROI (one source suggests hyper/hybrid titles aim for 30–80% ROAS by Day 3), implying perhaps **50%+ by Day 7** for very lucrative hits.  However, no puzzle-specific ROAS benchmarks by day were provided in the sources.  *In summary, puzzle games likely need retention above casual averages (D1≳30%, D7≳10%) and achieve modest ROAS (tens of % by D7); specific CPI and ROAS by country/genre were not found in connected reports.*

## 7. Solo dev with US\$100–1500  

No source explicitly addresses ultra-low-budget UA.  By inference: **Channels:** Low-budget ads (Meta Audience Network, TikTok Spark Ads, etc.) can be used, but reach will be limited.  Creative testing might be confined to a handful of ad sets ($5–10/day).  **Expected installs:** With \$1K, one might expect a few thousand installs at best (if CPI is low, e.g. \$0.10–\$0.50 per install).  **Conclusions:** Such small samples can estimate D1 retention (e.g. 1000 installs yields ~250 retained if D1=25%), enough to weed out obvious failures.  But D7 retention, ARPU, and ROAS will be statistically unreliable at that scale.  A lead gen blog suggests D1 below ~20% generally indicates product issues; a solo should heed that cutoff.  In short, a solo can use \$100–\$1500 to validate the **hook** (video CTR/IPM) and initial retention (D1) but should not draw firm ROI or long-term LTV conclusions without much more spend.  **No published source gives concrete numbers for solo budgets**, so these points are based on the soft-launch guidance and typical CPI assumptions.
